# CBDC deposit-outflow reading list

1. **Will Central Bank Digital Currency Disintermediate Banks?** (Whited, Wu & Xiao, 2022/2023)
   Takeaway: In a structural quantitative model, CBDC replaces bank deposits by roughly 80 cents on the margin, but bank lending falls by only about one-fourth of the deposit loss because banks substitute into wholesale funding and equity — so the deposit drain is real but the loan contraction is muted.
   SSRN abstract: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4112644

2. **CBDC, Bank Credit, and the Limits of One-for-One Refinancing** (Koka, 2026)
   Takeaway: A retail CBDC can displace commercial-bank deposits, and although the central bank can return the displaced funding to banks, refinancing is not one-for-one — outstanding deposits create a liquidity requirement and outflows add a settlement-date constraint, so lending capacity can still shrink.
   SSRN abstract: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7430418

3. **Central Bank Digital Currency and Bank Disintermediation in a Portfolio Choice Model** (Chang, Grinberg, Gornicka, Miccoli & Tan, IMF WP/23/236, 2023)
   Takeaway: In a calibrated portfolio-choice model, introducing a CBDC lowers aggregate deposits (about 4% of total wealth in the baseline) as imperfectly competitive banks raise deposit rates to defend funding, but bank lending falls only about 0.14% — deposit disintermediation barely transmits to credit.
   SSRN abstract: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4641122

4. **Central Bank Digital Currency and Banking Choices** (Li, Usher & Zhu, 2023/2024)
   Takeaway: A structural household-choice model shows a CBDC competes with deposit-taking institutions but only substantially crowds out bank deposits if it offers a materially better service network; a no-interest CBDC without complementary financial products leaves banks' deposit base largely intact.
   SSRN abstract: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4705512

5. **Central Bank Digital Currency and Financial Stability** (Kim & Kwon, 2019)
   Takeaway: In a monetary general-equilibrium model, interest-bearing account-based CBDC deposits reduce commercial banks' private credit supply and reserve-deposit ratios, raising bank-panic risk — unless the central bank lends the CBDC deposits back to banks, which then boosts credit and stability.
   SSRN abstract: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3330914
